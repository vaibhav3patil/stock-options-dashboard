What this application is

After the cash and F&O session ends, the desk answers four questions.

1 · Stock

A short High-priority list, plus a Top 5 to compare first.

2 · Side

BUY CALL or BUY PUT — you pay premium; you do not write options.

3 · Contract

Expiry, ATM / slight ITM / slight OTM, last-close premium, lot size.

4 · Plan

Research marks: Buy · Sell · Stop, margin %, reward-to-risk.

In scope

Indian stock F&O · long calls and puts · last-close scoring · High board · a simple management plan.

Not in scope

Live ticks · auto orders · writing options · index option ideas · a promise that High names will win.

A High-priority name can still lose the entire premium. This is a research board, not a broker and not advice. You place the trade yourself at the next open, at the live price of the printed strike and expiry.

The one idea the desk is built on

Option buyers pay premium and fight time. A name is only interesting when three things line up.

Large-enough move

The stock can travel farther than the option needs to break even.

Affordable premium

Implied volatility is not already “maxed out” for that move.

Usable contract

Enough activity near the money — not a cheap lottery ticket.

Liquid contract + affordable premium + a reason for a fast, large-enough move.

How you use it

An evening / pre-open ritual — not a day-trading terminal.

Open the desk→ Fast scan on / off→ Refresh scan→ Read the tape→ Compare Top 5→ Strict desk hint→ Open one name

How to take the idea

Buy that strike and expiry at the next session’s live price. Do not hunt a new ATM at 9:15 unless you mean to change the trade.

The full scanner of every name is hidden on purpose. Medium and Low ideas never appear on the board.

Hard gates first, then direction, then a contract, then a priority rank.

Spread only — do not buy naked

  • Options look rich vs recent actual movement, or
  • IV Rank is high (~50+ unfriendly; 70+ rich) unless the expected move is clearly larger than usual, or
  • An earnings / event sits inside the assumed hold (~2 sessions) — crush risk.

How CALL vs PUT is chosen

BUY CALL

Uptrend, or sideways but clearly stronger than Nifty over ~20 sessions.

BUY PUT

Downtrend, or sideways but clearly weaker than Nifty.

No tilt → no buy idea. A squeeze (the stock has been coiling) is a plus for buyers, but it does not flip the side by itself.

Which strike, breakeven, and cover

ATM

Closest strike to last close — preferred.

ITM-1

One strike in-the-money. Allowed if cover and cost look better.

OTM-1

One strike out. Harder BE. Strict desk rejects it.

Call BE = Strike + Premium   ·   Put BE = Strike − Premium
Cover = expected move (ATR over ~2 sessions) ÷ points needed to reach BE
Cost / lot = NSE board lot × premium
CoverReading
≥ 2.0×Strong — typical range has room
≥ 1.5×Healthy — the desk’s main bar
1.0–1.5×Tight — easy to miss
< 1.0×Typical move may never reach BE
Trade the printed strike and expiry. “ATM” on the card is last night’s ATM, not “whatever is ATM at 9:15”.

High priority and Top 5

Every remaining BUY CALL / PUT gets a potential from 0–100: how realistic is a path to profit? — not a promised return.

What lifts potential

Ready to buy · cover ≥ 1.5× (especially ≥ 2×) · a 1-ATR day still profits · livelier ATR% · ATM · squeeze + trend/RS aligned · easy need-to-BE · low IV Rank

What hurts potential

Watch / spread-only · cover below 1× · 1-ATR still below BE · OTM · need-to-BE ~3%+ · IV Rank 50–70 (worse above 70) · event within 5 days (worse within 2)

PotentialBandOn the page?

Below 50LowHidden

Top 5 = the first five High names. Gold is “look here first”, not “buy all five”.

Strict desk — what to actually buy

The compare board can show five names. Do not force five tickets.

Strict (live rule)

At most Top 2 · cover ≥ 1.5× · no OTM · IV Rank < 50 · no event in 2 days.
If you replay history: hold ~4 sessions; stop only on a closing print through the stop.

Wide (study only)

Old looser replay: Top 5, 2-session hold, stop on the day’s low. Use it to study history — not as the live buying rule.

If none of the Top 5 pass Strict, skip the session. Judge replays by average P&L, not win rate.

Buy · sell · stop

Research marks from last close, sized to the dated market tape on the banner. Not live quotes. Costs and slippage are not included.

Buy

Last-close option premium.

Sell

About 1.5× the risk, capped if a typical 1-ATR day cannot fund a bigger target.

Stop

In a cautious tape, about 32% of premium. Tighter when India is cautious vs the world.

Backtest — did last-close picks work?

Replay past sessions pretends you ran the desk on recent closes, takes High (or Strict) picks, and scores what that same contract did over the hold.

Signals

How many past closes were replayed.

Trades scored

How many had a later price to judge.

Win rate

Share that finished green — not the main score.

Avg P&L

The number that matters. A few losers can wreck a 50% win rate.

This is close-to-close research, not a live-fill backtest. A small sample is a check, not proof.

Word list

ATR / ATR%

Typical daily range, and that range as a % of close. Livelier names help buyers.

HV vs IV

How much the stock moved vs how expensive options are.

IV Rank

Where today’s IV sits vs its own history. Low = cheaper than usual.

RS

Vs Nifty. Leaders for calls, laggards for puts.

Squeeze

The stock has been coiling. Expansion would help a buyer.

Cover

Expected move ÷ need-to-BE. Above ~1.5× is healthier.

BE

Expiry break-even of the suggested strike.

Score

0–100 last-close composite (setup + liquidity + IV).

Potential

0–100 “can this option realistically get to profit?”

Easy ways to misread the desk

  • Treating High as “must buy five names”.
  • Buying a new ATM at 9:15 instead of the printed strike.
  • Ignoring cover and buying cheap OTM because the premium “feels affordable”.
  • Buying into rich IV the day before results.
  • Holding a weekly with no move and hoping time will help.
  • Forgetting lot × premium until after you are in.
  • Using the board as live prices.
Options can expire worthless. Data can be late or wrong. Nothing in this handbook is a recommendation to buy or sell any security.
